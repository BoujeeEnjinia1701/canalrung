# CanalRung user requirements

What the people in CNR-PRB-001 need, written from their side. The product requirements that meet these needs are in CNR-PRS-001 (`03-requirements.md`) and the links are in the traceability matrix (`03b-traceability.md`). Method: STANDARDS section 22. No user has been interviewed yet: the needs come from the problem statement and the sources it cites, and a co-design partner (CNR-PRB-001, Co-design) should confirm them.

> **Safety:** CanalRung is a research and educational design for rescue equipment, never certified life-saving equipment. Never enter the water to rescue someone: throw, anchor and call the emergency number.

Table 1. User requirements.

| ID | Category | Title | Need | Priority | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| UR-001 | Performance | Reach the person | A bystander can put something within the person's reach in a lined canal where the water is up to 2.0 m below the coping on a 1.5:1 slope, or 3.0 m on a vertical wall | High | Person in the water; Bystander on the bank (PRB Table 1) |
| UR-002 | Deployment and assembly | Quick and obvious to use | An untrained bystander gets it into the water in well under a minute, guided only by what is printed on the box | High | Bystander on the bank (PRB Table 1) |
| UR-003 | Usability | Climb out unaided | A person who is cold and tired can use it to climb a steep, slick wall without help | High | Person in the water (PRB Table 1) |
| UR-004 | Safety | Carries an adult | It holds a heavy adult climbing out, with a margin | High | Person in the water (PRB Table 1) |
| UR-005 | Safety | Stays attached to the bank | It does not pull free when a person holds on in a current | High | Person in the water; Bystander on the bank (PRB Table 1) |
| UR-006 | Safety | Harms no one | Nothing that is thrown or deployed can hurt the person in the water or the helper | High | Person in the water; Bystander on the bank (PRB Table 1) |
| UR-007 | Usability | One adult can handle it | A single adult, not necessarily strong, can lift and throw or place it | Medium | Bystander on the bank (PRB Table 1) |
| UR-008 | Performance | Within reach at the surface | The part the person grabs stays at the water surface and does not sink | Medium | Person in the water (PRB Table 1) |
| UR-009 | Environment and durability | Lasts outdoors | It stays ready through at least two years of sun, rain and frost without upkeep | Medium | Irrigation district and farm managers (PRB Table 1) |
| UR-010 | Cost | Cheap enough to place often | A district can afford a station at every crossing, pump house and path | Medium | Irrigation district and farm managers; Village committees and schools (PRB Table 1) |
| UR-011 | Maintenance and logistics | Built and mended locally | A village workshop can build and repair it from common stock and hand tools | Medium | Village committees and schools (PRB Table 1) |
| UR-012 | Maintenance and logistics | Inspected at a glance | An inspector can see in seconds that a station is complete and untouched | Low | Irrigation district and farm managers (PRB Table 1) |
| UR-013 | Regulatory | Honest about what it is | Anyone who finds it knows it is an open research prototype that is not certified life-saving equipment | Medium | Irrigation district and farm managers (PRB Table 1) |

Categories: deployment and assembly, performance, usability, safety, environment and durability, logistics, maintenance, cost, regulatory. Priorities: High, Medium, Low.
